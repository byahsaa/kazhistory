Genghis Khan · 1162–1227

One steppe, cut three ways

In a single generation the Mongol Empire swallowed the Great Steppe and then split it between four sons. Three of those shares fell across what is now Kazakhstan — and that division set the terms of its politics for the next three hundred years.

Part 011206

A man with no clan builds the largest army on earth

Genghis Khan was born around 1162 under the name Temujin. His father was poisoned, his clan scattered, and his family was left without protection — in steppe terms, without anything at all.

Over the next thirty years he reversed that completely. By war, by alliance, and by systematically dismantling the old tribal order, he bound the Mongol tribes into a single following. In 1206, at a kurultai — an assembly of the nobility on the Onon river — he was proclaimed Genghis Khan, supreme ruler of all Mongols.

Say this

Genghis Khan was born around 1162 under the name Temujin. His childhood was harsh: his father was poisoned, his clan fell apart, and his family was left without protection. But over thirty years he managed to unite the scattered Mongol tribes. In 1206, at a kurultai — an assembly of the nobility — he was proclaimed Genghis Khan, the supreme ruler of all Mongols.

Part 02Reform

His real weapon was an organisation chart

The decisive reform was the decimal system. The whole population was cut into tens, hundreds, thousands and tumens of ten thousand. A man no longer answered to the clan he was born into; he answered to the commander of his ten.

Two more reforms held it together. The Yasa was a single code of law binding on every subject of the empire, the khan's own family included. And promotion went by merit: a capable warrior could command regardless of his birth. Running through all of it was the yam, a chain of relay stations that moved a message across thousands of kilometres of grassland faster than any rival could react.

Say this

His strength lay not only in his army, but in his organization. He carried out three important reforms. The first was the decimal system: the whole population was divided into tens, hundreds, thousands, and tumens of ten thousand men. This destroyed the old tribal division, and now a man obeyed his commander rather than his clan. The second reform was the Yasa, a single code of law binding on everyone. The third was promotion by merit rather than by birth: a talented warrior could become a commander regardless of his origin. In addition, the yam was created — a postal relay network that allowed messages to travel enormous distances.

Part 031218

Otrar

A trade caravan was killed at Otrar. Then the envoys sent to demand an answer were killed too.
Inalchuq, known as Qaiyr Khan · governor of the city

For the Mongols this was not a commercial dispute. Killing an envoy was the gravest crime in their law, and it converted an ambition into an obligation. The western campaign had its justification.

Say this

The pretext for the western campaign was the event of 1218 in Otrar. The governor of the city, Inalchuq, known as Qaiyr Khan, ordered the killing of a trade caravan sent by Genghis Khan, and afterwards the envoys were killed as well. For the Mongols, killing an envoy was the gravest crime, and this gave a formal justification for war.

Part 041219

Two regions, two entirely different fates

In 1219 the army crossed into the state of the Khwarezmshah. What happened next was not uniform — and the difference is the part most often left out.

Southern Kazakhstan — broken

Otrar held out for roughly six months before it fell. The cities of the Syr Darya were burned, the irrigation systems that fed them were destroyed, and the population was killed or driven off.

Otrar · Sighnaq · Ashnas · Jend · Sauran · Yasi

Zhetysu — largely intact

Here the local rulers submitted almost without a fight. They had no loyalty to the ruler who had seized power over them shortly before the Mongols arrived, and they had no interest in dying for him.

Submission, not siege

Say this

In 1219 Genghis Khan's army invaded the state of the Khwarezmshah. Otrar held out for about six months and fell. Sighnaq, Ashnas, Jend, Sauran, and Yasi were taken. The cities of southern Kazakhstan were destroyed, the irrigation systems were ruined, and the population was either killed or driven away. Zhetysu, however, suffered far less: the local rulers submitted almost without a fight, because they were dissatisfied with their previous ruler.

Part 05Division

Three shares fall across one country

Genghis Khan divided the conquered lands among his sons during his own lifetime. The youngest, Tolui, took the Mongol homeland by custom. The other three shares landed on the territory of what is now Kazakhstan. Select a share to read it.

The eldest son

Ulus of Jochi

The western lands: from the Irtysh outward across Western and Central Kazakhstan and the whole Kipchak steppe. This is the share that mattered most for what came later — the Golden Horde grew out of it, and so, through the Ak Orda, did the Kazakh Khanate.

Say this

During his own lifetime Genghis Khan divided the conquered lands among his sons. The ulus of Jochi, the eldest son, received the western lands — from the Irtysh and further west, that is, Western and Central Kazakhstan. The ulus of Chagatai covered Southern Kazakhstan, Zhetysu, and Transoxiana. The ulus of Ögedei consisted of the north-eastern lands, the upper Irtysh and the Tarbagatai region. The youngest son, Tolui, received the Mongol homeland according to Mongol custom. As a result, the territory of modern Kazakhstan was divided among three uluses.

Part 06Ledger

What the division cost, and what it left behind

What it broke

  • Ties between regions that had taken centuries to form
  • The emergence of a single people and a single state — delayed by generations
  • The southern economy, once the Syr Darya cities and their canals were gone

What it built

  • Chinggisid legitimacy: only a descendant of Genghis Khan could be khan
  • That rule governed the Kazakh steppe until the nineteenth century
  • The ulus itself as a unit of government, inherited by every successor state
Say this

The consequences were twofold. On the one hand, the division broke the ties between regions that had been developing for centuries, delayed the formation of a single people and a single state, and the destruction of the cities along the Syr Darya undermined the economy of the south. On the other hand, the Mongols brought political principles that shaped the next three hundred years. The main one was Chinggisid legitimacy: only a descendant of Genghis Khan could be a khan. This rule operated in the Kazakh steppe until the nineteenth century. The ulus system of government was also inherited.

Part 07Line

The conquerors dissolved into the conquered

The Mongols were never numerous here. Within two or three generations they had been absorbed into the Turkic-speaking population, taking on its language and its customs. What survived was not a Mongol people but a Mongol idea of rule, carried forward by Turko-Mongol states.

The Ak Orda14th–15th c.
The Kazakh Khanatefrom c. 1465

The Mongol conquest did not erase the history of the steppe. It reorganised it inside a new frame. Read that way, the division of Kazakhstan among three uluses is not the end of its story — it is the opening of the next chapter.

Say this

It is important to understand that the Mongol conquest did not erase the earlier history of the steppe, but reorganized it within a new framework. The Mongols themselves were few in number, and within two or three generations they dissolved into the Turkic-speaking population, adopting the local language and customs. As a result, Turko-Mongol states emerged, and it was out of the ulus of Jochi, through the Ak Orda, that the Kazakh Khanate later grew. Therefore, the division of Kazakhstan among three uluses was not the end of its history, but the beginning of a new stage. Thank you for your attention.
